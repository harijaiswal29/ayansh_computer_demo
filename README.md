# Ayansh's Computer World

An animated, hands-on web app that shows a 7-year-old (Class 2) how a computer works, one building block at a time.

**To play:** download `index.html` and double-click it. It opens in any web browser (Chrome works best).
Nothing to install. It works offline too; you only need the internet for the nicer fonts.

## The 8 levels

| Level | What Ayansh does | What he learns |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Meet the Computer | Taps the monitor, CPU box, keyboard, mouse, speakers and printer. Each one comes alive. | The 6 parts and what each one does |
| 2. In, Think, Out | Types letters and watches each one travel from the keyboard, into the CPU box, out to the screen | Input, process, output, and how he does the same thing when he catches a ball |
| 3. Inside the Box | Opens the CPU box and explores the inside | CPU chip, RAM, hard disk, fan, power supply, motherboard |
| 4. Super-Fast Brain | Races the computer to count to 20 | A CPU does about 300 crore sums a second, but people have the ideas |
| 5. Desk and Cupboard | Makes a sticker picture, saves it, switches the power off and on, then opens it again | RAM forgets when the power goes off; the hard disk remembers |
| 6. Lights On, Lights Off | Flips a switch and sees every letter as 8 lights | Computers only understand ON (1) and OFF (0), shown with his own name |
| 7. Give the Orders | Uses arrow steps to guide a robot past rocks to a star | A program is a list of steps the computer follows exactly |
| 8. Big Quiz | Answers 8 picture questions | Goes over everything |

He wins a star for each level, and gets a certificate after all 8.

## Things to know

- **Bit the robot** reads every message out loud. Use **Read to me** to turn the voice on or off, and **Sounds** for the beeps.
- Colours always mean the same job: blue = IN, orange = THINK, green = OUT, yellow = STORE.
- Stars are saved in the browser. **Play again from the start** (at the bottom of the map) clears them.
- Works with a mouse, a touch screen or the keyboard (letters in Levels 2 and 6, space bar in Level 4, arrow keys and Enter in Level 7).

# Ayansh's Space Adventure

An animated, hands-on solar system in `solar-system/`. Nova, a friendly space guide, talks Ayansh through it in a recorded, natural-sounding voice, and every word she says is shown underneath so he can read along.

**To play:** keep the whole `solar-system` folder together (the voice lives in `solar-system/audio`, the planet pictures in `solar-system/tex`) and open `solar-system/index.html` in a web browser. Press **Blast off!** to start. It works offline; the internet is only needed for the nicer fonts. A computer or tablet from the last few years runs the 3D smoothly.

Everything is in real 3D with real planet pictures made from NASA images: Earth has its continents, clouds, shiny oceans and city lights on the night side. In Explore and in every 3D activity you can **drag to turn**, and **pinch, scroll or use the + and − buttons to zoom**, all the way from the whole solar system down to a planet's surface.

## The activities

| Tab | What Ayansh does | What he learns |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | Watches the planets go round the Sun in 3D, taps any of them (or the comet or asteroid belt) to fly there, zooms right in to the surface, or presses **Planet tour** | Each world's size, day, year, moons and a fun fact with a real-world comparison, plus a trick for remembering the order |
| Explore → **Visit as Ayansh** | Walks and jumps around that world in 3D in his space suit, with the world's real gravity, leaving footprints, and finds 3 or 4 glowing things to tap | What it's like to stand there: the sky, the gravity, the temperature, Valmiki crater on Mercury, the Apollo flag and Vikram lander on the Moon, blue sunsets on Mars, Saturn's rings across the sky, Pluto's heart and more |
| Explore → Earth → **Explore Earth** | Twelve activities (below) | How our own planet works |
| How big? | Slides along all the planets drawn at their true sizes next to the edge of the Sun | If Earth were a grape, Jupiter would be a watermelon and the Sun taller than a door |
| Weigh me | Sets his weight and age, picks a world, presses **Jump!** | Gravity: light on the Moon, heavy on Jupiter, and how old he would be in that world's years |
| Day & Night | Spins Earth and watches his home in India move into sunshine or night; then drags the Moon around Earth | Why we have day and night, and why the Moon changes shape |
| Space race | Races a car, a rocket and a beam of light to the Moon, Mars, the Sun, Jupiter or Neptune | How far away space really is |
| Missions | Flies ten real spacecraft in 3D (below) | How real space missions work |
| Quiz | Picks one of three games: the **Planet Quiz**, the **Space Adventure Quiz** (10 questions picked at random from 20, about the missions, the worlds and Earth) and **What If…?** | Goes over everything, with a star for each first-try answer |

### Explore Earth

| Activity | What Ayansh does |
|---|---|
| Globe | Spins a sharp 3D Earth (an 8,192-pixel NASA map, with mountain shading, clouds, shiny seas and city lights), taps continents, oceans and wonders, and uses **Fly to** to zoom right down to India, the Himalayas, the Sahara, the Amazon, the Nile, the Great Wall, the Pyramids, the Great Barrier Reef, the Grand Canyon, Niagara Falls, Mount Fuji or Antarctica. **Real time** lights the globe exactly as it is right now and tells him whether it's day or night in India |
| India | A real satellite picture of India with pins for famous places |
| Seasons | Watches tilted Earth go round the Sun, with the month, India's season, hours of daylight and how high the noon Sun climbs |
| Tides | Sees the Moon pull the oceans into bulges, and the water rise and fall on a Mumbai beach |
| Eclipses | Watches a solar eclipse from the ground, with day turning to night and the corona appearing, and a lunar eclipse turning the Moon red |
| Auroras | Sees the solar wind steered to the poles from space, then the green curtains from a snowy hill |
| Storm & rainbow | Counts the seconds between lightning and thunder to find how far the storm is, then brings out the Sun for a rainbow (VIBGYOR) |
| Cyclone | Watches a cyclone spin across the Bay of Bengal and zooms into its calm eye |
| Earthquakes | Pushes India north to raise the Himalayas, and shakes the ground while a seismometer draws the quake |
| Volcano, Rain cycle, Inside Earth | Makes a volcano erupt, follows rain from the sea to the mountains and back, and digs to the centre of the Earth |

### What If…?

Ten imagination questions, each with three choices. After the right answer, a 3D scene shows what would happen: the Sun disappearing (8 more minutes of light, then dark, frozen Earth drifting away), the Moon disappearing, Earth stopping spinning, Earth moving as close as Venus, gravity switching off, Earth having rings, Earth with no air, the Moon twice as close, Earth with no tilt, and shouting in space.

## Things to know

- Planet pictures: see `solar-system/tex/CREDITS.md`. The 3D engine is three.js, included in `solar-system/vendor`.
- **Voice** turns Nova on or off, **Sounds** controls the beeps. The two buttons beside her words replay or stop what she is saying.
- If a voice file is missing, Nova falls back to the best-sounding voice the browser has.
- Distances in the Explore view are squeezed so everything fits on one screen; **How big?** shows the true sizes.

## Changing what Nova says

Her lines are in `solar-system/lines.js`. After editing them, re-record the voice with [Kokoro](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx), a free text-to-speech model that runs on your own computer:

```bash
pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile
```

Download `kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` from the [model files release](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/tag/model-files-v1.0) into one folder, then run (ffmpeg is needed for the mp3 files):

```bash
python tools/make_voice.py --models /path/to/that/folder
```

Only changed lines are recorded again.
